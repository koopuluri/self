hey do the beats in the stream have timestmaps and identifiers and whatnot? how are the beats set up? what metadata is tracked?

> π: yes — every beat has a permanent numeric ID, and beats written in Stream have UTC timestamps down to the millisecond. the content and metadata live in two files:
> 
> - `<3/stream/index.md` holds the beats in document order. each is one logical line ending in a marker like `<!-- beat:1509 -->`. the ID isn’t a line number: beats can move without changing IDs, and retired IDs aren’t reused.
> - `<3/stream/metadata.json` holds the records keyed by those IDs. the app joins the two to display a beat.
> 
> what’s tracked:
> 
> | | fields |
> |---|---|
> | identity | author and kind: `text`, `asset`, `browser`, `document`, `chat`, or `bubble` |
> | timing | `createdAt`, `completedAt`, and, when edited, `updatedAt` (latest edit, not every edit) |
> | focus | `visits`: a list of `enteredAt` / `exitedAt` pairs. that tells you when the beat had focus, not whether you were reading it. |
> | provenance | imported beats can have the source path, line number, and import time, with the original creation time left unknown |
> | kind-specific details | for example, a browser URL, an asset’s name and path, or a π bubble’s chat ID and message index |
> | removal | deletion or redaction metadata; a retired ID can point to a surviving beat after a merge |
> 
> for example, beat `1509` — your “the stream itself becomes the data” beat — was created at `02:43:22.344Z` on nov 24, last edited at `02:45:12.254Z`, and completed at `02:45:12.726Z`. its record has seven focus visits, including returns after completion.
> 
> so for your status-component idea: the raw markdown tells you *what’s there and in what order*. the metadata adds timing and focus history, but there’s no general log of every state change. to learn “you picked the third option,” the component would need to record both the ranking you saw and the option you selected.

and for multiple beats when pasted together are they still treated as mulitple beats or a single beat? like this am i pasted like many paras from my notes, do those translate to multiplle beats or a single long one?

> π: multiple beats. when you paste several paragraphs into Stream, each paragraph gets its own beat and ID, assuming it’s one line in the copied text. blank lines between paragraphs don’t become beats.
> 
> the actual rule is line breaks, not paragraphs: a paragraph that only *wraps* on screen stays one beat, but a paragraph with hard line breaks can split into several. beats from the same paste can share a timestamp while still having distinct IDs.
