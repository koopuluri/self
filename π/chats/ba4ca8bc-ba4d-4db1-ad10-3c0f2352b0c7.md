> π: the distinction i’d put first: streaming naturally doesn’t mean saying everything that comes to mind. it means you don’t have to decide *where a thought belongs* before expressing it. you still decide *whether it’s worth expressing*. (beats 14–17, 593–595)

> here’s the first pass, grouped by what each principle guides. the labels are my synthesis; the quoted phrases are yours.
> 
> **while streaming**
> 
> 1. **practice right speech at capture and again at publication.** your test is “truthful, timely, useful, beneficial.” you don’t have to know in advance that a thought will prove useful; the point is to exercise judgment rather than blurt or censor by reflex. publishing gives you a second occasion to exercise it. (593–595, 1477)
> 2. **notice the difference between feeling and reacting.** anger, sadness, or excitement can be worth recording while you’re able to observe it. if it’s doing the speaking for you, let the mind settle first. “not being emotional does not mean becoming a rock.” warmth matters. (594–597, 1466)
> 3. **let the stream move forward.** a thought can trail off without becoming a task you owe yourself. if you return to it, that return is a new moment: record what you see *now* rather than trying to resume exactly where you left off. (598, 745)
> 4. **redact rather than rewrite.** withholding something is different from making it appear that something else happened. that applies even to a timestamp. correct metadata if it’s wrong about reality, not because another sequence tells a better story. (979–980)
> 
> **working with π**
> 
> 5. **you follow the thought; π connects the dots.** a question shouldn’t begin with choosing a chat, finding an old thread, or filing a note. say it here. the stream preserves the context; π can recover relationships and derive organization later. (14–17, 99, 169, 1228)
> 6. **“bias toward less instruction. bias toward NO instructions.”** express what you notice or want before translating it into a procedure for π. delegate the small choices you don’t care to make; keep judgment over what you want and why. (521, 695–696)
> 7. **protect the writer’s attention, not the agent’s momentum.** if π needs an answer, that can block π without interrupting you. let questions accumulate until *you* reach a natural break. likewise, an event can belong in a log without deserving a beat in your stream. (478–479, 972)
> 
> **deciding what to work on**
> 
> 8. **“structure must evolve after we have actual substance.”** an idea can live in the stream until it “earns” a document. the same goes for a directory or a whole app: start with an actual need, not a model of everything you might eventually need. (14–15, 693, 735, 1473)
> 9. **spend a fresh mind on the work that needs one.** the concrete example was choosing another walk over going home to implement. figuring out *what was worth building* still needed your freshest attention; implementing a design you could already see could wait. (926–929)
> 10. **don’t use speed of shipping as a substitute for judgment.** sitting with the proposed workspace long enough to discover a simpler stream was productive work. the distinction isn’t fast versus slow; it’s whether building now serves the work or merely relieves the anxiety of not having shipped. (712, 1068–1070)
> 11. **have an objective without craving the result.** curiosity doesn’t need to justify each step, but attention is scarce: eventually you decide whether to keep going. the razor’s edge names the other half: “if you crave reaching the end of the razor you lose the awareness of the moment you require to not fall off where you are.” (1394)
