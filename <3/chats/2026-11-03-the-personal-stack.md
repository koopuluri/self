A **manifesto is a declaration of what you believe and what you think should follow from those beliefs**. It usually names something about the present that needs to change, offers a different vision, and invites people to act or see differently. It can be personal, artistic, technological, or political.

**Yes, [your piece](/Users/karthikuppuluri/code/ghost/expression/personal-manifesto.md) qualifies.** It has the essential shape:

- **A conviction:** artificial intelligence will never render the human spirit irrelevant.
- **A diagnosis:** we’ve lost touch with our humanity, and AI makes recovering it urgent.
- **A proposal:** a new “personal stack” of personhood, personal AI, and personal stock.

The declarative language, the repeated “we will need,” and the closing “Onward” all give it the character of a manifesto. Its brevity works for that form.

The part I’d reconsider is **“personal.”** “My personal manifesto” usually suggests *the principles I choose to live by*. Yours makes a broader proposal about how people should thrive. “Personal” makes sense because the individual is the organizing unit of your vision, but a reader won’t necessarily know that from the title.

My suggestion:

> π: **The Personal Stack**  
> A manifesto for thriving in the age of superintelligence.

That gives the piece a distinctive name while making its ambition clear.

Other labels would change the expectation: **“a thesis”** invites readers to examine an argument; **“a framework”** invites them to use an organizing structure; **“notes on…”** signals ideas still taking shape. **“Manifesto”** asks readers to engage with a conviction and a proposed direction.

I’d keep *manifesto* as the description and give the piece its own title.
